### Valid analytic statements

In Section 1 write `D(v)=6-2d(v)+p(v)` and `m(v)=3-s(v)`.
The angle count gives the following upper bounds:

| Case | `D(v)` at most | `m(v)` |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `s=3` | `3-d <= 0` | 0 |
| `s=2` | `4-d <= 2` | 1 |
| `s=1`, `d>=2` | `6-d <= 4` | 2 |
| leaf | 4 | 2 |

Thus `D <= 2m`; if `d>=3`, `D <= 3m/2`. The face accounting gives
`sum D = 12 + sum_(l>=6)(l-6)`, proving the asserted bounds. The facial-walk
argument also covers bridges and, for at least four vertices, trees.
`C5` with colours `(0,1,2,3,1)` and `C6` with `(0,1,2,0,1,2)` attain six.

The C1/C2/C3 discharging inequalities hold. For a possibly disconnected plane
graph, face lengths count all boundary components and the initial total is
`-6(1+c)`, not `-12`. Keeping the given embedding avoids assuming that an
isolated component's face remains a face after other components are inserted.
For U1/U2, the definition of bad
pairs and the discharging argument are checked against
[Bartier et al., Sections 3.2–3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.00631#S3.SS3),
especially the definition before Lemma 3.15 and Lemmas 3.17–3.22. Extracting
the unavoidable set is a structural consequence of those charge estimates;
it does not import their recolouring reductions as Hamiltonian reductions.

### C1 exception and the leaf projection

The constant boundary `(0,0,0,0,0)` leaves a 3-colouring graph of `C5` with
30 states in two components of size 15. Since a single face was explicitly
called a pentagon tree, both the universal connectivity sentence and the
proposed theorem for **all** pentagon trees fail. A valid future statement
must treat C1 separately or work with components.
There is also a direct invariant: identify its three allowed colours with
`Z/3Z` and write oriented cycle differences as `+1` or `-1`. Their sum forces
the number of `+1` edges to be either one or four. A legal recolouring swaps
two adjacent opposite signs, preserving that number; both classes occur.

For a leaf path `x1-x2-x3-x4`, take outside colours `(3,0,0,2)` and junction
colour `a`. Its lists are
`[4]-{a,3}`, `[4]-{0}`, `[4]-{0}`, `[4]-{a,2}`.
For `a=0,1,2,3` the numbers of path states are `11,17,23,23`.
At `a=0`, `(1,2,3,1)` is isolated. This refutes the uniform-bundle
interpretation, not a prospective induction with additional invariants.
The residual base must omit the constraints through the deleted leaf
vertices; fixing those newly exposed neighbours defines a different base.

### Actual face unions outside the finite models

The following are complete oriented face lists. Opposite directed edges
pair up, every vertex has a single cyclic rotation, the graph is connected,
and `n-m+f=2`. Consequently they specify spherical embeddings. The small
checker also verifies girth five and the stated opposite angles.

**Extra internal edge in a bowtie.** Use faces

```text
(0,1,2,3,4)
(0,5,6,7,8)
(2,1,0,8,7)
(2,7,6,5,0,4,3)
```

The first two faces are opposite at the degree-4 vertex `0`. The graph has
9 vertices and 11 edges, including `2--7`, absent from the enumerated
bowtie. The face lengths are `5,5,5,7`. Thus even a bowtie's induced union
need not equal the bare face union generated by either constructor.
An additional exact check of this example finds no failed connectivity or
non-killability: this is a coverage counterexample, not a counterexample
to either property.

**Identifications between branches of a U1 certificate.** Use faces

```text
(u,a,b,c,d)
(u,g,f,v,e)
(v,j,b,a,h)
(u,e,v,h,a)
(u,d,c,b,j,v,f,g)
```

There are 11 vertices and 14 edges. The first and second faces are opposite
at `u`; the second and third are opposite at `v`. The two leaf faces share
the edge `a--b`. They therefore do not form the 13-vertex abstract
three-face chain. After the completion below, the second face is U1:
`u` and `v` are bad via the two leaf faces and its other vertices have
degree three.

These examples extend to graphs of minimum degree three. For each degree-2
vertex, attach a bridge in a complementary face to the subdivision vertex
of an edge of a fresh dodecahedron. Each attachment is planar, introduces
no new cycle through the bridge and makes both endpoints degree three.
The selected first two/three faces stay facial. The resulting graphs have
respectively `(n,m)=(135,203)` and `(158,238)`, minimum degree three and
girth five. Thus the hypothesis `delta>=3` does not remove either gap.

### Conditions needed for restoration

Non-killability must apply to every actual bottom move, including all
neighbours of a shared port simultaneously. Two abstract killing moves
isolate a component along a bottom cycle only if both are legal and are
that cycle's two incident moves. The new component-walking lemma additionally
assumes a connected whole layer; the article's two-factor argument can
reset its factors at different layers.

Path splicing is sufficient if one has a simultaneous assignment of
Hamilton cycles with two actual shared internal edges at every consecutive
interface, together with both cross edges. Separate pairwise choices of
cycles do not establish that invariant. Disconnected C1 fibres require
component-level splicing. No such Hamiltonian material is established by
the new connectivity scans.

The remaining degree-2 patterns agree with the article's adjacent-pair,
`(2,3,2)`-path and separated-pair reductions. For at least two surviving
vertices, their two-element neighbourhoods pairwise intersect. Such a family
either has a common hub or is the three edges of a triangle; girth five
excludes repeated neighbourhoods. A lone degree-2 vertex is a separate case.
