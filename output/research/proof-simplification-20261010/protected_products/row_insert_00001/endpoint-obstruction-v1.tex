\paragraph{A second obstruction survives either one-edge repair.}
The balance identity forces both $YY$ edges into the cut matching
and the new-row forest. Their four endpoints are therefore already
used by the matching and form two isolated $K_2$ components of the
new-row forest.
Suppose the protected set still contains
\[
 13210\,23210,\qquad13230\,13232,\qquad13230\,23230.
\]
Then $13230$ has protected degree two in the new-row forest.
The complete neighbourhood of $13210$ in the forty-four-state
component is
\[
 \{13212,13213,13230,23210\}.
\]
The first two vertices belong to the forced $K_2$ components, and
the third already has protected degree two. Hence $13210$ has only
its protected edge to $23210$ in the new-row forest, so it must be
an endpoint of the cut matching. It cannot be matched to either
already-used $YY$ endpoint, to the degree-two vertex $13230$, or
through its protected edge to $23210$. This is a contradiction.
Thus every matching insertion, for every possible two-row seed
and every number of cuts, must omit at least one of these three
protected edges. Neither a change of reserve pair nor a longer
path search removes this obstruction.
