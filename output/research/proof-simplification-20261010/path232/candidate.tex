\paragraph{A single bad layer.}
For each side $i\in\{L,R\}$ let $S_i$ be the colours $c\ne a$
whose square $Q_c$ has an available edge in its chosen good cycle.
An end change supports all three squares; a middle change supports
the two squares other than its changed colour, with at least two
available edges in each. Thus $|S_L|,|S_R|\ge2$ and
$S_L\cup S_R=[4]\setminus\{a\}$: the only possible exception would
be two middle changes to the same colour, which would give $t=s$.

We can choose a common square $Q_c$ with different available edges
on the two sides. If either change is at the middle, choose any
$c\in S_L\cap S_R$; that side offers at least two edges.
If both changes are at ends, with changed colours $b_L,b_R$, choose
$c\notin\{a,b_L,b_R\}$. The available singleton edges in $Q_c$
are different: changes at different ends give different coordinate
directions, while changes at the same end have $b_L\ne b_R$ and
give opposite edges of the square.

Start with the two good cycles and the three bad squares.
Attach $Q_c$ to both good cycles using the two different edges just
chosen. Attach each remaining square to either good cycle that
supports it. These four incidences form a tree on the five cycles.
Cuts at a good cycle occur in different middle-colour squares, so
they are distinct; the two cuts at $Q_c$ are distinct by construction.
Ordinary edge splices along this tree produce one spanning cycle,
and no reserved edge is cut.
