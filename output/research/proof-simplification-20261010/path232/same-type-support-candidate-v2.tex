\paragraph{Two additional support arguments.}
Let $a,b,c,d$ be distinct colours. For the pair of end-change types
$t=(a,a,b)$, $s=(a,b,b)$, consider the common square in the layer
with middle colour $c$. Its opposite corners are $bca$ and $dcd$.
The two selected cycles retain the same two square edges incident
to $bca$. Their respective special edges are incident to $dcd$
and each is absent from the other cycle. Thus the two edges at
$bca$ are common and safe. This also holds with $c,d$ interchanged.

For the all-distinct pair $t=(a,b,c)$, $s=(a,d,c)$, the layer with
middle colour $a$ has the common hexagon
\[
H=(bab,bad,cad,dad,dab,cab,bab).
\]
The source cycle retains five edges of $H$. Interchanging $b,d$
preserves $H$ and gives the target cycle, which also retains five
edges of $H$. Hence the two cycles have at least four common edges.
All are safe because all-distinct cycles have no special edges.

The displayed square and hexagon properties follow from the
three-grid construction, or directly from its three cyclic words.
Unused-colour interchange preserves the end-change cycle, and the
chosen all-distinct family has one edge set. Therefore these
arguments cover every allowed cycle at both boundaries. Colour
permutations and path reversal give all labelled versions, so they
remove the $001\to011$ and $012\to032$ orbits from the table.
