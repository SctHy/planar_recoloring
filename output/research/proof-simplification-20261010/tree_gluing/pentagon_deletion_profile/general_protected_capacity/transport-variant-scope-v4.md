# Transport variants and one genuine uniform capacity family

The thirty relative hard-boundary groups contain fifty-eight hard-side incidences from the original fifty-three interfaces. The local study checks only the at-most-seventeen fixed-support edges against transported membership in the already listed opposite protected bases. It generates no new cycle and no complete fibre. It does not compute the original paired hard/other protected-edge intersection. It does compute intersections among the *opposite transport variants* to determine whether one local retained subset works for every recorded variant; this is separately declared in the JSON.

Only eighteen of the thirty groups have identical local retaining subsets. Nineteen have identical coordinate-count vectors, so vector equality is insufficient for equality of edge sets. For example, `00112` changing coordinate 3 to 0 has two different transported local subsets, both with vector `(2,1,1,0,1)`. A neighbour-boundary table that silently identifies these images would therefore be invalid.

Twenty-six groups have a single coordinate with retention exceeding its fixed surviving missing-edge budget in every recorded variant. Twenty-four groups have the stronger property that the common opposite-variant port core itself exceeds that budget. The two remaining count-positive groups have empty cores: `00001` changing coordinate 4 to 0 leads to the two disjoint constant components; `00112` changing coordinate 4 to 0 has four transported `00011` bases. In the latter group the missing-edge budget in coordinate 3 is zero and every variant retains at least one of the two ports `21301–21321`, `32301–32321`, but the same port is not retained by all four variants. This is exactly why count-based capacity can work without equivariance or a common fixed witness.

Allowing a fixed union of coordinates increases the uniform-group coverage from twenty-six to twenty-seven. The added group is `00112` changing coordinate 2 to 0. Its two opposite transports have retention vectors `(1,1,1,0,0)` and `(2,2,0,0,0)`; neither a single coordinate nor the common opposite-port core yields the generic missing-edge bound. In the union of coordinates `{0,2}`, both variants retain two edges and the missing budget is one.

The candidate `transport-variant-capacity-candidate-v4.tex` proves this family directly: the four-edge support contains three hard protected edges; each of the two prescribed transported `00012` protected cycles retains at least two edges. Those two retention claims follow from just three protected ports in the one listed `00012` cycle and their explicit coordinate/colour maps. Thus `3+2−4=1` gives a common protected edge for all three recorded interfaces of this family. This is a local analytic proof with an explicit two-map quantifier, not a theorem for arbitrary Hamilton cycles.

There is a shorter proof for exactly this family. The hard protected set contains `e=13230–23230`. Its images under the identity and `h(w)_j=p(w_(2-j mod 5))`, with `p` exchanging 1 and 2, are `13230–23230` and `13103–13203`. Both are literal consecutive edges of `C1-00012`, and neither lies in `POOL-00012-0`. These two memberships establish a designated common port for the two restored selected bases. The common opposite core does not meet the generic missing-edge budget, but its named edge `e` is itself a hard protected edge. This explains why the direct proof succeeds where the generic core-count bound fails. It establishes neither equality of the transported bases nor equivariance of their pools.

The independent checker `transport_family_check_v4.py` reads the frozen literal blocks without importing the original v4 implementation. It reconstructs only the hard `00112` twenty-endpoint support to identify the four-edge set, checks the two maps and the three named ordinary ports, and reads the already produced v4 result to bind the three interfaces. The original thirty-group v4 computation and the earlier 313-interface computations are not rerun. Its exact result is `transport-family-check-results-v4.json`. The named-port membership check is additional evidence distinct from enumerating an original paired hard/opposite protected-edge intersection, which remains unexecuted.

The original physical witness is already `12320–32320` for all three interfaces. The coordinate comparison is:

| Frozen interface | Old witness in hard coordinates | New witness in physical coordinates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `00012:0`, position `0→2`, target `00112:0` | `31201–31301` | `13103–13203` |
| `00012:0`, position `2→1`, target `00112:0` | `12320–32320` | `13230–23230` |

Thus a uniform hard-coordinate port is a useful analytic family, but adopting its physical witnesses would replace one shared old literal by two new literals. No printing-length gain for these three actual entries has been established.

The three relative groups still not uniformly positive under coordinate-union missing budgets are `00001` changes 4 to 2 or 3, and `00112` change 0 to 2. They contain the two hard self exceptions of v3. The fact that other actual interfaces can be covered using the opposite hard side should not be confused with positivity for every variant on one chosen hard side.

This study identifies a real mechanism—uniform retention in a coordinate union can survive variant changes when no single coordinate or generic common-core count meets its budget. For the added family, however, the designated-port proof just stated is shorter than the capacity argument. The remaining twenty-six successful groups are still a finite classification of the recorded port counts, and their retention hypotheses have not been uniformly derived. Reprinting them would not remove mathematical cases. The original fifty-three-case protected system remains the delivery baseline; no manuscript was edited.

The v4 computation record and manifest bind both the retained comparison and the new narrow execution. The standard record validator establishes schema, safe relative paths and hashes only; it does not replay a computation, authenticate the prior execution, or review the mathematics. No claim for arbitrary Hamilton cycles, arbitrary normalizers, or all adjacent hard boundaries is made.
