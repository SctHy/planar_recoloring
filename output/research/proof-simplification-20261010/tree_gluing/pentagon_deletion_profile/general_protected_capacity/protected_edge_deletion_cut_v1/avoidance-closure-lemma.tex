With $K,B,P_B,S,H$ as in the deletion lemma, suppose a Hamilton
cycle $C$ of $H$ exists. Maintain an allowed edge set $A$ containing
$C$ and a forced edge set $F\subseteq C$, initially $A=E(H)$ and
$F=\varnothing$. At a vertex with exactly two incident allowed
edges, both are forced. At a vertex with two incident forced edges,
every other incident allowed edge is excluded. These implications
preserve $F\subseteq C\subseteq A$. Hence fewer than two allowed
edges or three forced edges at a vertex is impossible. A proper
cycle in $F$ is also impossible: its vertices already use both
$C$-edges inside that cycle, contradicting that $C$ is spanning and
connected. Any such finite implication certificate proves that
$H$ has no Hamilton cycle and thus every Hamilton cycle of $B$
uses a safe fixed-$K$ edge. A stable implication set without one
of these obstructions does not assert that $H$ has a Hamilton cycle.
