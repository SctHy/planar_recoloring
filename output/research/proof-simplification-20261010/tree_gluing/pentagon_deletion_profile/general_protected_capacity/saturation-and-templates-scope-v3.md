# Fixed saturation, dynamic saturation, and a bounded template route

The canonical shortcut is a property of the fixed full hard support `J_t=G[V(K_t)]`: `00001` coordinate 1 and `00112` coordinate 2 have no missing edge. The coverage 32/53 (10/30 constant-source) checks only whether the opposite protected basis has a retained port there, transporting a target coordinate by `s+εk`. In contrast, “any hard-side saturated coordinate” 46/53 and “either-side saturated coordinate” 50/53 were computed *after restricting to the interface-specific* `W=V(K)∩V(L)`. They are dynamic count conditions, not additional fixed template symmetries. The fourteen best scalar profiles are finite numeric profiles, not proved symbolic families.

The fixed supports have twenty endpoints and seventeen edges; their six missing edges are listed in `hard-support-candidate-v3.tex` and the result JSON. Their full coordinate missing budgets are `(1,0,1,2,2)` and `(1,2,0,2,1)`. For an adjacent opposite boundary, delete illegal endpoint vertices. The resulting missing budget depends only on the hard template and the relative changed boundary, not on the opposite cycle. If the opposite protected basis retains more ports in one coordinate than this remaining budget, then at least one of those ports belongs to the hard protected set. This is a proved conditional set statement and does not assume the opposite basis is a Hamilton cycle.

The opposite retention counts inherited from the W record are **exact counts in this fixed legal support**, not just lower bounds. Indeed, for an opposite protected edge, having both endpoints in `V(K_t)` automatically puts them in `V(K_t)∩V(L)`, because every edge of `L` has both endpoints in `V(L)`. Thus `L∩E_j(J_t(u))=L∩E_j(H[W])`. This identity permits exact reuse of the frozen v2 counts without reading opposite cycle edge sets again.

Without deleting boundary-illegal endpoints, this fixed missing-edge criterion covers 44/53; after the deletions it covers 51/53. The 51 inequalities remain finite selected-basis evidence. The two failures are hard self interfaces: `00001:0,4,3→00001:0` and `00112:0,0,2→00112:0`. Their six-vertex joint supports are respectively `P2+4K1` and `P3+3K1` (the latter's two edges share the centre `13023`). Each protected basis retains all the one/two edges of its support, so capacity finishes these two specified interfaces. No protected-edge intersection was computed.

The 21 cases missed by the fixed canonical shortcut are stored with exact case parameters and hard-coordinate opposite boundaries in `fixed_preferred_failures_21`. Their relative parameter sets are:

- `00001`: `(r,q)=(1,1),(1,2),(1,3),(3,3),(4,0),(4,2),(4,3)`, giving opposite boundaries `01001,02001,03001,00031,00000,00002,00003`.
- `00112`: `(r,q)=(0,2),(2,0),(2,2),(2,3),(4,0),(4,3)`, giving `20112,00012,00212,00312,00110,00113`.

These are the parameter sets encountered in the frozen 53 standard-source interfaces, not assertions for every possible target map or Hamilton cycle.

The 53 interfaces give 58 hard-side incidences (five have a hard component on both sides). They collapse to thirty canonical hard-boundary changes. Nineteen changes have one retained coordinate profile, ten have two, and one has four. The corresponding relative normalizer multiplicities are the same. This prevents treating the relative boundary alone as specifying the chosen protected-port profile. In particular, `00112` changing position 4 to 0 has four inherited profiles, while `00001` changing position 4 to 0 includes the two constant component labels. The grouping records both the relative normalizer and component label, rather than silently identifying these choices.

The current analytic gain is a two-template missing-edge budget and two small self-support certificates. A genuinely shorter replacement for all 53 old witness cases still needs a proof that one of the coordinate retention inequalities always holds in the selected opposite templates, respecting the recorded directions. Merely printing the 51 positive scalar inequalities would exchange one finite table for another. This branch therefore does not recommend integrating a new 51-row table into the manuscript.
