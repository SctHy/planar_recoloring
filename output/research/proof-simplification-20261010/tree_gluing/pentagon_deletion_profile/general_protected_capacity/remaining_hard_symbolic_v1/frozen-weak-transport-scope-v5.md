# Named-port transport families and the limits of regrouping

This study reuses the existing thirty-group v4 local retained-edge lists. It does not rerun their producer or any earlier 313-interface computation. For candidate generation, each of the eleven protected ports of each hard template is tested against the saved local lists. Twenty-eight groups admit a fixed hard-coordinate port retained by every recorded variant. The exceptions are `00001` changing coordinate 4 to 0 (the two constant components) and `00112` changing coordinate 4 to 0 (four different `00011` transports). This is a finite candidate classification, with no whole-template or pool-equivariance claim.

The executed named-edge verification then reads the actual frozen literal bases and pools only for three selected families. It checks 23 transformed edge memberships, covering 14 boundary groups, 31 hard-side incidences and 30 distinct interfaces of the original fifty-three. One hard self interface has two incidences. No full original paired protected-edge intersection, full fibre, Hamilton-cycle search or new normalizer is computed.

The two fixed-port selections are:

| Hard type and fixed port | Relative changes `(coordinate,new colour)` | Hard incidences | Distinct interfaces | Distinct opposite image memberships |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `00112`, `13230–23230` | `(0,3),(1,2),(2,0),(2,3),(3,0),(4,1),(4,3)` | 14 | 13 | 10 |
| `00001`, `21230–23230` | `(0,1),(0,3),(2,1),(2,3),(3,1),(3,2)` | 12 | 12 | 9 |

The maps and literal images for every selected group are preserved in `weak-transport-families-results-v5.json`. These parameter sets were obtained from the recorded variants. They are not all legal adjacent boundaries and do not follow from equality of a coordinate-count vector. In particular, the first port is legal under nine adjacent changes, but is retained by all recorded opposite maps under only the seven listed changes: `(1,1)` and `(3,2)` are legal but fail. The retained-port classification therefore depends on the selected opposite bases and pools, not legality alone.

An inspectable analytic subfamily of the first selection has three relative changes: `(2,0),(2,3),(3,0)`, with opposite boundaries `00012,00312,00102`. Each supplies three actual interfaces. Their prescribed maps are, respectively, `{id,h2}`, `{p,h1}`, `{id,h1}`, where `h_a` reverses coordinates about `a` and exchanges colours 1 and 2, and `p` cycles colours 1,2,3. The single hard port `13230–23230` needs just six cycle/pool memberships. The candidate proves these nine interfaces by the named-port transport criterion.

The third selection is a genuine two-by-two parameter family. For hard `00112` and boundary `00110`, the four maps to `00011` are the rotation with start 4 or the reflection with start 1, followed by either identity or the colour exchange 2 and 3. Use hard port `A=21301–21321` for rotations and `B=32301–32321` for reflections. The four images are `12130–12132`, `13120–13123`, `23103–23123`, `32102–32132`; all are literal edges of `C1-00011` outside its pool. This proves five interfaces. The common local retained subset of all four variants is empty, so a single fixed port cannot cover the family; the orientation-dependent choice is part of the statement. This gives a concrete analytic reason for transport stability despite absence of a common opposite core.

The two short analytic candidates together cover fourteen distinct interfaces. Their proof is in `weak-transport-candidate-v5.tex`. It quantifies only over the stated finite map families. The larger two fixed-port selections are useful finite classification, but merely replacing the old witness entries with all their map and image memberships would not by itself establish mathematical simplification.

The old/new physical-witness comparison is saved for every selected interface. Across the thirteen-interface `00112` fixed-port selection the number of distinct physical witness literals changes from five to four; across the twelve-interface `00001` selection it changes from seven to four; for the orientation family it stays five. These are only counts of distinct edge literals. They do not measure the cost of the required normalizer data, maps, protected membership justifications or proof exposition, and no rendered-page or textual-size improvement has been established. The source/target roles also matter: a fixed hard-coordinate port need not be the same physical word in a source-side table.

The primary selected-template certificate and the main manuscript remain the baseline. This branch writes only research candidates and evidence. The standard computation record validator checks schema, safe relative paths and hashes; it neither executes this calculation nor reviews the mathematical argument. Exact finite membership evidence does not prove arbitrary Hamilton cycles have the required ports, nor that any selected cycle or pool is equivariant.
