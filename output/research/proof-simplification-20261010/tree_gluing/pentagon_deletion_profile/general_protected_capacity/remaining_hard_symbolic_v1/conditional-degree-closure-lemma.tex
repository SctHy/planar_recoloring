\paragraph{Conditional degree closure.}
Fix a protected set $K$ on one side, and let $G$ be the full opposite
component with pool $Q$. Put $S=(K\cap E(G))\setminus Q$.
If a Hamilton cycle avoids $S$, all its edges start in
$A=E(G)\setminus S$. At a vertex with two allowed edges both are forced;
at a vertex with two forced edges every other edge is excluded.
These implications preserve every such cycle. A vertex with fewer than
two allowed edges or more than two forced edges, or a forced cycle on a
proper vertex subset, is impossible. Any such obstruction proves that
every Hamilton cycle meets $S$. An obstruction-free closure is inconclusive.
The fixed-$K$ side needs no Hamilton cycle.
