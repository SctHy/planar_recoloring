\paragraph{Capacity on joint port support.}
Let $K=A\setminus P$, $L=B\setminus Q$, and write $V(K)$ for the
endpoints of edges in $K$. Put $W=V(K)\cap V(L)$, and let $H'$ be
the legal recolouring graph induced on $W$ inside the common component
state set. Every common protected edge has both endpoints in $W$.
For a coordinate class, put
\[
x=|K\cap H'_j|,\quad y=|L\cap H'_j|,\quad m'=|H'_j|.
\]
Inclusion--exclusion gives at least $x+y-m'$ protected common edges
in this universe. One may also remove
$F'=(P\cup Q)\cap H'_j$. With $f'=|F'|$,
$r_K=|K\cap F'|$, and $r_L=|L\cap F'|$, the bound becomes
\[
x+y-m'-r_K-r_L+f'.
\]
This is the previous edge-set lemma applied to $H'$; the intersection
of the clipped safe sets remains $K\cap L\cap H'_j$.

The refined bound cannot be smaller than the refined full-component
bound in the same coordinate. An edge removed by restricting to $W$
cannot belong to both protected sets. If it survives the pool exclusions,
its contribution to the full-universe capacity sum is therefore at most
zero; if it is excluded by a pool, its contribution is zero. Deleting
these contributions can only increase the bound.

Neither $W$ nor $H'$ is asserted to cover the full fibre. In particular,
the hard fibres have $44$ and $51$ states, whereas either protected
$K_{11}$ has only $20$ endpoint states. A positive numerical bound
still requires its selected-basis counts to be proved for any proposed
symbolic family; finite kernel coverage alone is not that proof.
