\paragraph{Sound forced-star closure.}
In a graph known to have a Hamilton cycle, initially allow every edge and
force none. At each vertex, force its two allowed incident edges when only
two remain, and exclude every other incident edge when two are forced.
Iterate. Every Hamilton cycle contains the forced edges and uses only
allowed edges: induction follows from its degree two at every vertex.
This implication does not infer Hamiltonicity from a surviving closure.
For two components sharing $x$, let $\mathcal T_s(x),\mathcal T_t(x)$
be the incident two-edge subsets of their final allowed stars containing
all incident forced edges. For fixed pools $P,Q$, every pair of Hamilton
cycles has at least
\[
\min_{U\in\mathcal T_s(x),\ V\in\mathcal T_t(x)}
 |(U\cap V)\setminus(P\cup Q)|
\]
common safe edges at $x$. The minimum only involves local two-edge
choices; those choices need not extend to whole Hamilton cycles.
