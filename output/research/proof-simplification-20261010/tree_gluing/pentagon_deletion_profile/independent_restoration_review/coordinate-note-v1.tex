\paragraph{A coordinate-capacity argument for one pentagon interface.}
Consider $t=00101$ and $s=10101$, with physical positions $0,\ldots,4$.
The canonical target normalizer is $(s_0,\epsilon,p)=(0,-1,(0\ 1))$,
so its restored cycle is $g(C_t)$, where
\[
 g(x)_j=p(x_{-j\bmod5}),\qquad p=(0\ 1).
\]
Let $J$ be the common recolouring edges changing position $0$.
Their endpoint colours are $2,3$. Both neighbours must therefore
avoid $2,3$; the unchanged lists force $x_1=1$ and $x_4=0$.
Thus every edge of $J$ has the form
\[
 21xy0\text{--}31xy0,\qquad
 x\in\{0,2,3\},\quad y\in\{1,2,3\},\quad x\ne y.
\]
There are $9-2=7$ such edges. The involution $g$ preserves $J$ and
sends the pair $(x,y)$ to $(p(y),p(x))$.

The printed $64$-state cycle uses five of these seven edges, at
\[
(x,y)=(0,1),(0,2),(2,1),(2,3),(3,2).
\]
This follows directly from its consecutive first-coordinate changes.
Consequently the restored target cycle also uses five edges of $J$.
The source pool meets $J$ only at $21010$--$31010$, and $g$ fixes
this edge. The union of the source and restored target pools therefore
removes only one edge of $J$. Each protected cycle retains at least
four of the six remaining edges, so they have at least
\[
 4+4-6=2
\]
common protected edges in $J$. This replaces the individual witness
for this interface without requiring whole-cycle or whole-pool
equivariance.

\paragraph{Scope and limitation of the whole-graph bound.}
This is a proof for the existing selected Hamilton cycles and pools,
transportable by their common coordinate and colour maps. It makes no
assertion for arbitrary Hamilton cycles or for other interfaces.
Here the entire common graph has $40$ vertices and $87$ edges.
Each outside set has eight cycle components (six $P_2$ and two $P_6$),
and the pool union contributes three common-graph edges. The coarse
whole-graph deletion lower bound is
$80-16-87-3=-26$, hence is inconclusive. Restricting the capacity count
to a coordinate layer is the substantive step.
