\begin{lemma}[Fixed vertices of a preserved Hamilton cycle]
Let $g$ be an automorphism of a finite graph $X$, acting nontrivially
on $V(X)$. If $g$ preserves the edge set of a Hamilton cycle of $X$,
then $g$ fixes at most two vertices. Consequently, if $g$ fixes at
least three vertices, no Hamilton cycle of $X$ is $g$-invariant.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Label the vertices in their cyclic order by $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$.
Every cycle automorphism has the form $i\mapsto a+i$ or $i\mapsto a-i$:
the image of a vertex and one of its neighbours determines all images.
A nonidentity rotation fixes no vertex. A reflection fixes the solutions
of $2i=a\pmod n$, at most two. Since the cycle is spanning, $g$ acts
nontrivially on its vertex set.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Products with fixed physical rows]
Suppose $g$ acts nontrivially on $V(A)$ and fixes $f$ vertices there.
Its action $g\times\mathrm{id}$ on $A\square P_r$ fixes exactly $rf$
vertices. If $rf\ge3$, no Hamilton cycle of this product is invariant
under that action.
\end{corollary}
This obstruction requires a nonidentity induced action on states, not
merely a nonidentity description of a boundary map. Combined actions
that also permute the physical rows require a separate fixed-point count.

\paragraph{Hand example: $0000\ast$.}
Let $R(x)_i=x_{3-i\pmod5}$ and $\tau=(1\ 2)$, fixing colours $0,3$.
Both preserve this boundary. The natural stabilizer is
$\Gamma=S_3\times\langle R\rangle$, of order $12$: the star fixes
the coordinate axis, and the repeated excluded colour fixes $0$.
A state fixed by $g=\tau R$ has the form
\[
       (a,b,\tau(b),\tau(a),d).
\]
Properness forces $b\in\{1,2\}$ and $d\in\{0,3\}$.
For either $b$, choose $a$ to be the other transposed colour, with
two choices for $d$, or choose $a=3$, with $d=0$.
Thus there are exactly six fixed states:
\[
 12120,\ 12123,\ 21210,\ 21213,\ 31230,\ 32130.
\]
They are among the $54$ legal states, so $g$ is nonidentity and no
Hamilton cycle can be invariant under the full $\Gamma$.
All three transpositions give the same six-fixed-vertex obstruction.

The fixed-point test leaves two order-six subgroups as the largest
possibilities: the pure colour $S_3$, and
$\langle(1\ 2\ 3),R\rangle\cong C_3\times C_2\cong C_6$.
Every nonidentity element of either has no fixed legal state.
This is a necessary-condition result; it does not construct an invariant
Hamilton cycle for either subgroup.

\paragraph{Hand example: the hard type $00112$.}
Take the same $R$ and let $\sigma=(0\ 1)$, fixing $2,3$.
The boundary stabilizer is $\{1,\sigma R\}$. A fixed state has the form
$(a,b,\sigma(b),\sigma(a),d)$. Its list at position $4$ and fixed-colour
condition force $d=3$. Properness at positions $1,2$ forces $b=1$;
then properness next to $d$ and the list at position $0$ force $a=2$.
The unique fixed state is $21023$.
The three-row product therefore has three fixed vertices. No chosen
$A\square P_3$ seed can be invariant under this full stabilizer acting
with the physical rows fixed. The two-row product has two fixed vertices
and is not excluded by this criterion.

For comparison, the type $00001$ has stabilizer $C_2\times C_2$;
$R(2\ 3)$ fixes the four states
$12310,13210,23230,32320$. Its full stabilizer cannot preserve any
two- or three-row Hamilton seed.

\paragraph{Exact scope across the twenty-one nonconstant types.}
The natural action is
$x_i\mapsto p(x_{s+\epsilon i})$, with $s\in\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z$,
$\epsilon=\pm1$ and $p\in S_4$, restricted to maps preserving the
boundary. All nonconstant components are connected, so this is also
their natural component stabilizer. Exhaustive state enumeration gives
faithful induced actions in every case. In the table, $f$ lists the
fixed counts of nonidentity elements, including multiplicity.
$h_r$ is the largest subgroup order not excluded by the fixed-point
test on $A\square P_r$; $r=1$ denotes $A$ itself. Subgroup closure
was enumerated exactly; no Hamilton cycles were searched.

Nine types have an obstruction to a full-stabilizer-invariant cycle,
thirteen to a full-stabilizer-invariant two-row seed, and fifteen to a
three-row seed. The eight Hamiltonian types among the first nine already
exclude a globally equivariant choice of a single component cycle.
The two hard types exclude a globally equivariant choice of all specified
seeds with rows fixed. An equivariant family containing several cycles
or seeds is not excluded; for each affected type its orbit must contain
at least two choices by the orbit--stabilizer formula.

\paragraph{No conclusion about invariant protection alone.}
The obstruction concerns the entire chosen Hamilton cycle. It says
nothing by itself about a proper protected subset $K$. For example,
in $K_5$ the transposition $(1\ 2)$ fixes three vertices and preserves
no Hamilton cycle, while the nonempty protected set $\{34,45\}$ is
invariant and lies on a Hamilton cycle. Thus the present counts do not
prove that an invariant $K$, or an equivariant protection-only system,
is impossible. They show why requiring every specified cycle and seed
to respect the full natural stabilizer is too strong.
