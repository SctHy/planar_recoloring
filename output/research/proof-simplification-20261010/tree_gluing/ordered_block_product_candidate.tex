\begin{lemma}[Ordered small path blocks]\label{lem:pentagon-ordered-blocks}
Each of the 23 standard pentagon components $B$ has a spanning path
factor whose paths have order two or three and can be ordered so that
every two consecutive paths are joined by an actual edge of $B$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Twenty-one components have the specified Hamilton cycles. The component
$00112$ has the printed Hamilton path. In each, divide a Hamilton path
into consecutive pairs, using one triple if its order is odd.
For $00001$, use the 22 pairs in Table~\ref{tab:matching-chain-00001}.
They are legal adjacent pairs and contain all 44 states exactly once.
For $i=1,\ldots,21$, the table specifies the edge from $a_i$ if
$\epsilon_i=0$, or $b_i$ if $\epsilon_i=1$, to $a_{i+1}$.
Each such pair differs in exactly one coordinate, so the factor paths
occur in the required order.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Replacement proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:protected-product}]
Order the $P_2/P_3$ blocks of $B$ as above. For each block construct its
product cycle with $A$: join copies of $C_A$ using the first two pool
edges when $A$ is Hamiltonian, and otherwise use its specified two- or
three-row seed. Every cycle retains $K_A$ and the last six pool edges
in every physical row.

Fix two of those reserve edges. Process the ordered blocks, joining
successive product cycles by deleting the two copies of one reserve
edge in the rows supplied by their actual $B$ joining edge and adding
the two Cartesian cross edges. Alternate the two reserve edges at
successive interfaces. A block participates in at most two joins,
which use distinct reserve edges even if they fall in the same physical
row. Therefore each selected edge is still present when used.
Every switch joins two disjoint cycles into one and preserves all
copies of $K_A$. Induction gives the required Hamilton cycle.
\end{proof}
