\paragraph{Safe internal paths and nine interface families.}
Let $a,b,c,d$ be four distinct colours. Write $uvw$ for the assignment
$(u,v,w)$, and let $[x_0,\ldots,x_r]$ denote the edge set of a path.
Only edges with fixed middle coordinate are used below.

For boundary $aab$, the safe internal edges of the end-change cycle
are precisely the four paths
\[
[cba,cbd,dbd],\quad[dba,dbc,cbc],\quad
[dca,bca,bcd],\quad[cda,bda,bdc].
\tag{E}
\]
The other two internal edges are the special edges and are excluded.
For boundary $aba$, define orientation $0$ to be the middle-change
cycle whose two root-grid splice edges change the last coordinate;
orientation $1$ is its coordinate reversal. The internal edges of
orientation $0$ are
\[
\begin{gathered}
[bac,cac,cad,cab,dab,dac,dad,bad],\\
[bcb,dcb,dcd,bcd],\qquad[bdb,cdb,cdc,bdc].
\end{gathered}
\tag{M}
\]
For orientation $1$, reverse the coordinates in every assignment.
For the all-distinct boundary $abc$, the internal edges are
\[
[bab,bad,cad,dad,dab,cab],\quad
[bcb,dcb,dca,dcd,bcd,bca],\quad
[bda,cda,cdb].
\tag{A}
\]
These identities follow directly from the three-grid splices: each
path is the portion of a grid cycle left after cutting its splice
edge or edges. All displayed edges are safe. Swapping unused colours
preserves (E) and (M); reversing coordinates sends (A) to the rule for
the reversed boundary, because its all-distinct cycle is unique.

For adjacent good boundaries, orient the transition from the smaller
type in $E<M<A$, and reverse coordinates if needed to put an end
change at the side shown. The two safe common edges can be chosen
by the following symbolic rules:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|l}
\text{source}&\text{target}&\text{M orientation}&\text{two common edges}\\\hline
aab&aac&--&cba\!\text{--}\!cbd,\ bca\!\text{--}\!bcd\\
aab&abb&--&bca\!\text{--}\!dca,\ bca\!\text{--}\!bcd\\
aab&bab&0&cba\!\text{--}\!cbd,\ dba\!\text{--}\!dbc\\
aab&bab&1&cbc\!\text{--}\!dbc,\ cbd\!\text{--}\!dbd\\
aab&acb&--&cba\!\text{--}\!cbd,\ cbd\!\text{--}\!dbd\\
aab&cab&--&dba\!\text{--}\!dbc,\ dca\!\text{--}\!bca\\
aba&cba&0&dab\!\text{--}\!dac,\ dac\!\text{--}\!dad\\
aba&cba&1&bcb\!\text{--}\!bcd,\ dcb\!\text{--}\!dcd\\
abc&abd&--&dca\!\text{--}\!dcb,\ cda\!\text{--}\!cdb\\
abc&adc&--&bab\!\text{--}\!bad,\ bad\!\text{--}\!cad
\end{array}
\tag{F}
\]
The orientation is that of the target cycle in the $E$--$M$ rows,
and that of the source cycle in the $M$--$A$ rows, after the chosen
coordinate reversal. Every entry follows by substitution in (E),
(M), or (A), including coordinate reversal for a left-singleton
end-change cycle.

The remaining family is $aba\to aca$. The two cycles each retain
three edges of the same square with middle colour $d$ and endpoint
colours in $\{b,c\}$. Their retained edge sets therefore intersect
in at least two edges, for either orientation of either cycle.
These edges are safe because middle-change cycles have no special
edges. This square argument eliminates the relative-orientation
certificate for the $M$--$M$ transition entirely.

To see that the rules are exhaustive, an $E$--$E$ transition either
changes the singleton colour or changes the middle colour to the
singleton; an $E$--$M$ transition changes the repeated endpoint to
the singleton. An $E$--$A$ transition changes either the middle or
the repeated endpoint to an unused colour. An $M$--$M$ transition
changes its middle colour, while an $M$--$A$ transition changes an
endpoint to an unused colour. Finally, an $A$--$A$ transition changes
an endpoint or the middle to its unused colour. These are nine
families; only $E$--$M$ and $M$--$A$ need the orientation parameter.
Thus (F) and the square-intersection argument prove the safe-edge
lemma without numerical edge indices or twenty target-image entries.

\paragraph{Editorial tradeoff.}
The parametric proof exposes the grid mechanism and supplies ten
explicit edge-pair rules for nine families. Displaying all internal
path identities makes it longer than the frozen twelve-entry table.
The twelve-entry version remains the shorter manuscript option;
this version is appropriate if the aim is to replace an index
certificate by a hand-checkable structural argument.
