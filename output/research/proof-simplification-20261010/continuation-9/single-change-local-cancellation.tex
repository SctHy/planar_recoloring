\paragraph{Cancellation for one changed forbidden colour.}
Suppose two full listed recolouring components differ only by changing
the forbidden colour at coordinate $j$ from $a$ to $q$.
Let $v$ be legal in both, and let $d_s,d_t$ be its two degrees.
Put $A=\mathbf1[a\notin\{v_{j-1},v_{j+1}\}]$, and let $B$ indicate
that recolouring $v_j$ to $q$ is a legal source move.
Source legality gives $a\ne v_j$, and common legality gives $q\ne v_j$.
The target allows the source-forbidden move to $a$ exactly when $A=1$,
and removes the source-allowed move to $q$ exactly when $B=1$.
All other incident moves coincide, so
\[
d_t=d_s+A-B,\qquad d_c=d_s-B.
\]
If $f$ common incident edges lie in the two fixed pools' union, the
arbitrary-two-Hamilton-cycle local bound reduces to
\[
\max(0,4-d_s-d_t+d_c-f)=\max(0,4-d_s-A-f).
\]
Thus source degree three suffices when $a$ is a neighbour colour and
the common incident star avoids both pools. Source degree two permits
one pool edge when $a$ is a neighbour colour, and otherwise requires
pool avoidance. This condition still requires both full components
to have Hamilton cycles and does not assert an arbitrary cycle retains
all pool edges used elsewhere for splicing.
